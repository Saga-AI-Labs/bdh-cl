## 1. Executive Summary

This paper uses BDH, a byte-level depth-recurrent language model with an append-only, zero-initialized growth rule, to argue that continual learning decomposes into three separately measurable concerns — **storage** ("*Storage* is exact for the tested growth construction: under gradient-masked growth, previously acquired computation remains bit-identical at the tensor level across every subsequent phase"), **serving** (joint summation-readout degradation, of which "83% of the damage on the log scale, 62% in perplexity units, is reproduced by appending a single random, untrained block"), and **addressing** ("label-free likelihood selection achieves 20/20 correct prefix identification at every calibration budget from 4 KB to 1 MB"). The central thesis — that the open problem in continual learning is "*stable, efficient **addressing** of the accumulated territories … not destructive forgetting*" — is supported by an unusually disciplined measurement arc with pre-registered predictions (including a reported FAIL and a REFUTED), multi-instrument triangulation, and candid disclosure of withdrawn results and data contamination. Headline arithmetic is almost entirely reproducible from committed artifacts (I re-derive 0.833/62%/4.1×/4.3%/8.0% exactly), with one genuine numeric error (the f32 decay-offset claim does not reconcile its own quoted values), several scope-and-label inconsistencies (four different "acquisition band" values quoted under one name), and reproducibility gaps (an operator table present in the TeX but dropped from the Markdown sync; a named raw data file not in the repository). Overall this is a strong, honest measurement paper whose weakest points are statistical power (single seed, n=6 OOD probes fit and evaluated on the same set) and a disclosed-but-unresolved architecture-vs-objective confound.

## 2. Research Objectives

**Stated purpose.** The paper "reports a measurement arc that separates three concerns" that "the continual-learning literature usually conflates":

1. **Storage** — "*Under masked growth, is previously acquired computation physically preserved? We answer at the bit level for the tested configurations: yes*".
2. **Serving** — "*Given preserved storage, does the model still serve old tasks?*" — decomposed into *joint* (all neurons active) and *routed* (prefix-masked) regimes.
3. **Addressing** — "*Can the model find the right territory without an external language ID?*"

**Guiding hypothesis (the thesis).** Quoted verbatim: "*BDH provides an append-only neural substrate in which previously acquired computation remains physically intact while new computation is added. The central research problem is stable, efficient **addressing** of the accumulated territories—byte-separable in the regime measured here, and the open question for domains whose statistics are not—not destructive forgetting.*"

**Falsification clause.** The paper pre-commits to its own refutation criteria: "*Three results, none observed: (i) masked growth failing to preserve a phase bit-exactly (the P5 protocol would catch it); (ii) a *fixed* readout operator repairing joint serving (seven tested, all refuted, two vacuously); (iii) selection failing at moderate territory counts with a unimodal NLL surface*".

**Pre-registered predictions.** The register (Table 1) fixes 15 predictions with outcomes including one outright **FAIL** ("*P-FCS-3 … **FAIL**; family structure is stronger and more specific*"), one **REFUTED** (P-R1b), one **NEGATIVE** (P-R1), one **PARTIAL** (P-X3), and one honestly **NOT STARTED** (P-R4, conformal thresholds). A register that preserves failed predictions is methodologically exemplary and is the paper's strongest process asset.

## 3. Methodological Evaluation

### 3.1 Research design

The design is a **measurement arc** rather than a single benchmark run: a fixed-capacity forgetting baseline (Section 4), a growth ladder under gradient-masked additive growth (Section 5), a mechanistic decomposition of readout damage (Section 6), and a selection/OOD suite (Section 7), plus a cross-script generalization cell (Section 8). Each claim class gets its own instrument (bit-equality checks, cold-crop perplexity matrices, routing diagnostics, random-expansion controls), and — rarely seen in this literature — the instruments are *cross-validated against each other*: "*three independent instruments (torch c-fits, optimizer-moment census, weight-atlas spectral fingerprints) agree to four decimals*". **Strength:** the dissociation of storage / serving / addressing / support is a genuine methodological contribution ("*each with its own instrument … We offer these instruments to the CL literature as portable methodology*").

**Strength — controls are real and load-bearing.** The random-expansion control is the paper's best single experiment: three arms (random block, trained block, inert block) with the decisive negative control "*inert blocks cost exactly nothing (bit-identical at every width), which simultaneously validates the row-layout handling and proves the damage requires nonzero contributions*". The multi-era replication of this control "*reproduce[s] the English result independently first (`f_log`=0.832 against the published 0.833 …) — which is what licenses comparison with the number already in this paper*" is textbook replication discipline.

**Strength — confound discovery and repair.** The decay confound section is the most valuable part of the paper methodologically: a derived closed form ("*masked weights shrink by the schedule product `c = ∏(1 − lr_t·wd)` … the per-phase factor is `c=0.8927`*"), matched "*to four decimals*" against measurement, repaired with a step-end restore ("*Confirmed independently on two seats, two hosts, and two scripts … `c=1.000000` exactly*"), and the repair's cost quantified ("*8.7 ms per GiB restored at the final width (2.06 GiB snapshot, 17.9 ms against 3038 ms/step, 0.59% of step time*"). I independently verified the restore-overhead figures against `docs/reports/2026-09-12_pi-50_restore-overhead.md`: they match (8.7 ms/GiB; lt row 2.062 GiB, 17.9 ms, 3038 ms, 0.59%).

**Strength — failure transparency.** The withdrawn byte-addresser fit is disclosed with its cause ("*the first fit (76% agreement …) was withdrawn by its author after he found a composition confound*"), and the Inuktitut contamination is reported with both contaminated and corrected numbers ("*Both numbers are reported; the correction is a worked example of the second-seat cross-check discipline*"). The pre-registered falsifier on the seventh era is explicitly triggered ("*the pre-registered falsifier triggered on the seventh*", f_log 1.472 at el).

### 3.2 Data sources, sample size, statistical power

**Data.** Europarl for the 20-language ladder, MultiUN v2 for Chinese ("*a fresh 100M BDH … trained on 30 MB of Chinese (MultiUN)*"), OPUS for Inuktitut. All evaluation is "*per-domain held-out perplexity under a fixed cold random-crop protocol (block 512, 40 crops, generator 1234), always within-corpus*".

**Power is the paper's central statistical weakness, and the author knows it** ("*single seed per run (measured seed floor 2–4%)*"). Specific consequences:

- **Effective n for routing claims is 20 domains × 40 correlated crops**, and the paper correctly refuses the inflated reading ("*crops within a domain are not independent, so the raw 800 should not be read as 800 independent trials*"; Wilson floor "[0.91,1.0]" for a perfect 40/40 — I recomputed the Wilson lower bound: 0.91 ✓; likewise the 8/8 floor of 0.68 ✓). But with one seed, the Wilson interval understates total uncertainty: it covers crop sampling, not seed or ladder-order variance.
- **The retention headline (+4.3% median routed cost) has n=20 domains but n=1 ladder, n=1 ordering.** A single run cannot separate "routing cost" from "this ladder's idiosyncrasy"; the paper's own limitations acknowledge this ("*one language ordering per ladder, with order effects observed only implicitly through the family structure*").
- **OOD thresholds are fit and evaluated on the same n=6 probe set** — the paper says so plainly ("*they are fit and evaluated on the same `n=6` probe set; the separation they produce is measured, but it is not held-out validation*"). Any reader quoting "all 20 trained languages pass and all 6 unseen reject" as validated generalization is misreading; the paper does not, but the abstract's structure invites it.
- **The 2k-step re-acquisition probe (Δ=1.8% "below the 2–4% seed floor")** is n=2 arms × 1 seed. The conclusion ("*the once-trained, 19-times-overwritten state contributes *nothing measurable* to re-learning speed*") is properly hedged as "*resolution-bounded*", but "forgetting is not access loss" (used as a headline in the intro bullet) is stronger than a 2-arm, fixed-budget probe can carry. The authors' own caveat ("*fixed-2k endpoints cannot separate 'destroyed' from 'intact but slowly re-accessible'*") should demote the headline.

**Fatal flaw? No — but one confound is close.** The comparison that anchors the whole narrative (fixed-capacity floor = destruction vs. growth ladder = preservation) "*moves two variables at once: the capacity mechanism *and* the route-aware auxiliary loss (α=0.9 …), which is present for the growth phases and absent for the floor*". The paper discloses this in Open Problems ("*therefore cannot separate how much of the recovered routed-serving competence is bought by the architecture and how much by the objective*") and proposes the isolating arm. Because the storage claim (bit-exactness) is architectural and unaffected by the confound, and the serving claims rest on within-ladder comparisons, the confound does not invalidate the paper's three-way decomposition — but it does mean the *headline contrast* (forgetting vs. preservation) is not yet causally identified. This is the single most important experiment to run next.

### 3.3 Analytical framework

The theory section is appropriately scoped: Theorems 1–2 and the corollaries are proved in Appendix 15, and measured-vs-proved status is labeled throughout ("*`measured/heuristic` … the argument is not written out here*"). Two formal defects:

- **Theorem 1's proof shifts the injection through depth without justification.** "*every old-task trajectory shifts by `c` per level: `F'^L(x)=F_A^L(x)+Lc1≠F_A^L(x)`*" treats `F' = F_A + c1` as if its iterates accumulate `+c` per level. But `F_A^2(x) = F_A(F_A(x)+c1)` generally differs from `F_A^2(x) + c1` unless `F_A` is 1-Lipschitz-affine in the additive direction; the `Lc` accumulation line presumes translation equivariance that is not assumed. The *existence* claim is easy to repair (define `F'` to add `c` at every level application by construction, or use constant trajectories), but as written the proof line does not hold. The theorem's conclusion is almost certainly true; the proof is not valid as written.
- **Theorem 2's statement is malformed** in both language and TeX: "*`F'^L(x)=F_A^L(x)` for all `x∈X_A`, `0≤ℓ≤L` **iff** …*" mixes `L` and `ℓ`, and the proof's forward direction silently quantifies over truncations ("*The hypothesis at `L=ℓ*` gives …*") that the theorem statement does not provide (the statement quantifies only at the terminal depth `L`). The induction in the backward direction is fine. Minor repairs, but a theory paper cannot ship theorem statements with free indices.

Remark 1 ("*Under *soft* activity, LayerNorm's global statistics break (C1) at first order in the suffix magnitude … the argument is not written out here*") is honestly labeled and backed by `leakage.pdf`; acceptable.

**Present and working:** all four `data/` files verify against `figure_manifest.json` SHA-256 hashes ✓; the figure scripts (`scripts/quinn/rev5_paper_figures.py`) and training/eval scripts are in-repo; figures are regenerated "*from the committed A4 routed-cost table and the final RA2b matrix*"; pre-registration headers are said to live "*in the training scripts*"; every headline number I checked traces to a dated `docs/reports/` artifact.

1. **The seven-operator table is missing from the Markdown.** The TeX has the full table (`\begin{tabular}` with absK 39.4/79/1160/287, ev-shift 36.7/…, massnorm 31.26/39.77/91.77/215073/180510, softmix 18.73/27.24/68.31/4486/4093, …), but the Markdown contains only the footnote ("*The two identity controls below come from different instruments … so they differ by an order of magnitude*") followed by **no table**. §6.2's readers on the Markdown/PDF-Markdown path cannot see the primary evidence for "seven fixed readout operators, all refuted". 3. **`rev4-ai-disclosure-draft.md` is not "*committed with this revision*"** — the AI-participation section says it is, but the file exists only at `docs/papers/rev4-ai-disclosure-draft.md` from the previous revision and no rev5 copy is in `docs/papers/rev5/`.

**Reproducibility verdict:** numbers are *traceable* (exemplary report culture) but not *reproducible end-to-end* from the rev5 directory alone. A skeptical artifact bundle (raw matrices + run scripts + pinned environment) would take this from "trust the dated reports" to "re-run the numbers".

## 4. Key Findings & Evidence

Each finding below is anchored in verbatim quotes; my independent verification status is noted.

**F1 — Storage is bit-exact under gradient-masked growth.**
> "*under gradient-masked growth, previously acquired computation remains bit-identical at the tensor level across every subsequent phase (verified independently across two seats, two hosts, and two scripts, including cross-script)*"

and the P5 protocol held "*at every tested transition … masked-cell optimizer moments `v≡0`, old-segment tensor equality `c=1.000000` … Frozen segments do not move. Storage is exact.*" The scope qualifier is stated up front ("*`bit-exact` refers to equality of the preserved parameter tensors and buffers, not to identity of every floating-point output*"), which is correct and necessary. Cross-script instance: "*the zh segment is bit-identical across Hindi growth in encoder, value-encoder, and decoder (first cross-script instance of the bit-exactness protocol)*". **Verified:** consistent with the decay report's `c=1.000000` claims; tensor-level equality is an artifact check, appropriately scoped.

**F2 — The decay confound: a real, derived, and repaired optimizer defect.**
> "*AdamW's decoupled weight decay multiplies every parameter that has a gradient by `(1−lr_t·wd)` each step. A gradient mask that zeroes the frozen weights' gradients does *not* stop this … under the default lr schedule … the per-phase factor is `c=0.8927`; under the cosine schedule … `c=0.5798`*"

Verification: "*residuals ∼10⁻⁵ across 1-, 4-, 18-, and 19-phase intervals, three independent instruments … agree to four decimals*", and the repair gives "*`c=1.000000` exactly*". I verified 0.8927/0.5798, the three-instrument agreement, and the restore cost (8.7 ms/GiB etc.) against `2026-09-04_decay-family-two-regimes-and-boundary-repair.md` and `2026-09-12_pi-50_restore-overhead.md` ✓. The one **numerical error** in the paper lives here (the f32 offset sentence — see §3.4): the claim "*a deterministic offset of `−1.4×10⁻⁶` per plateau phase … which reconciles the measured 0.892636 with the exact-precision 0.892752*" does not reconcile (gap is 1.16×10⁻⁴ and the source report computes the offset per *step* over ~9,700 plateau steps ≈ 1.3×10⁻⁴ ✓). The qualitative finding survives; the quoted constant and its unit are wrong. Precedent claim is well hedged ("*has been observed independently in production MoE training (Marin project tracker, thread 8818) … suggesting a failure class, not an idiosyncrasy*").

**F3 — Forgetting under fixed capacity is family-structured destruction, not recency, and not mere access loss.**
> "*Row 20 … *nine fully displaced*—serving at or above their English-only zero-shot level … *seven partial retention* (pl 0.34×, sl 0.35×, cs 0.37×, sk 0.43×, ro 0.61×, hu 0.78×, et 0.87× their zero-shot); and the two non-Latin scripts collapsed four orders of magnitude at row 20 (bg 18,613, el 10,928)*"

Note the abstract says "*collapse by four orders of magnitude*" while the source report says "*five orders of magnitude*" (×12,086 ≈ 4.1 orders for bg; ×6,873 ≈ 3.8 for el) — "four orders" is defensible as a floor but inconsistent with the cited report's own summary line. The P-FCS-3 FAIL is reported honestly: "*Pre-registered prediction: the most recent phase dominates backward interference. Measured: the en column oscillates between 10.5–13.6 ppl after Romance/Germanic phases and 22.1–29.1 after Slavic/Uralic phases, with a Germanic phase *partially restoring* en after a Slavic one (29.1 → 11.3)*" — mechanism proposed: "*shared Latin-script byte statistics act as implicit replay*". The two-arm probe conclusion "*Δ=1.8%, below the 2–4% seed floor: the once-trained, 19-times-overwritten state contributes *nothing measurable* to re-learning speed*" is verified numerically (1.67/1.70, Δ=1.8% ✓) and properly caveated ("*the claim is resolution-bounded*").

**F4 — Routed serving tracks acquisition; joint serving does not.**
> "*routed perplexity at the final checkpoint exceeds each domain's own acquisition exit by a median of `+4.3%`, worst case `+8.0%` (hu)*"

vs.

> "*Joint full-width serving on the final chain recovers dramatically vs. the leaky era (bg 1649 → 230, el 891 → 64) *without any repair*—but non-lt domains still serve 1.0–37.8× above acquisition (median 11×)*"

I reproduced the +4.3%/+8.0%/−0.8% and 11.4×/37.8× statistics exactly from `a4_routed_cost.csv` and `figure_manifest.json` ✓. The zero-drift claim is strong and precisely scoped: "*The p19 and p20 routing diagnoses … are *bit-identical* on all 19 non-lt domains' routed perplexities—zero drift across a full growth phase, against `+88%` (fi) and `+21%` (hu) in the leaky era*".

**F5 — Joint degradation is mostly readout arithmetic; no fixed operator repairs it.**
> "*appending a single random, untrained block costs English `4.1×` … random expansion to full width reproduces 83% of the real damage at the English-era checkpoint (log scale; 62% in linear perplexity) … inert zero-blocks cost nothing*"

Recomputed: 9.57/2.33 = 4.11 ✓; ln(20.16/2.33)/ln(31.07/2.33) = 0.833 ✓; (20.16−2.33)/(31.07−2.33) = 0.62 ✓. The multi-era replication "*reproduces the English result independently first (`f_log`=0.832 against the published 0.833 …)*" and the honest scoping "*the mechanism generalizes across eras; the fraction does not … span `0.599` (sl) to `1.472` (el) … the pre-registered falsifier triggered on the seventh*" are verified against the A7 report ✓. The operator sweep conclusion: "*There is **no language-agnostic scalar reweighting of the BDH readout that repairs growth damage***" — with the decisive calibgain arm: "*the fitted vector converges to base territories ≈2.4, appended territories ≤0.12 (several at the clamp floor)—that is, the optimizer *rediscovers oracle masking*, specialized to the language it was fitted on*". Note the over-claim in the intro bullet ("*fails to repair it*") is self-corrected one clause later ("*we do not claim the space of input-independent operations is exhausted*") — the discipline is present but the sentence structure front-loads the strong version.

**F6 — Label-free selection is robust but not sublinear.**
> "*20/20 domains route to their own prefix at every calibration budget from ∼4 KB (8 crops) to 1 MB*"

and the failure mode:

> "*binary search over prefix widths … collapses to 4–6/20, because the cumulative-prefix NLL surface is **multimodal** … 14–16 land in a wrong local minimum. The failed assumption is the finding: selection scales linearly in territory count unless a cheaper addresser exists below it*"

Routing rigor is carefully stated: "*this is an online-routing protocol, not a held-out split … the observed routing is therefore *oracle serving* for this benchmark*", with a disjoint-crop confirmation ("*the fixed route still resolves to the true acquisition width for all 20 domains*") cited to `2026-09-13_quinn_tier1-router-split-report.md` (the citation string is itself corrupted in the PDF — see §3.5).

**F7 — Byte-geometry addressing reproduces the likelihood router in-support, and the two instruments disagree informatively out-of-support.**
> "*under a **class-balanced fit**, agreement is 20/20 domains at 1.00 each (8 held-out crops per domain; the per-domain Wilson floor for a perfect 8/8 is `[0.68, 1.0]` …), at ∼4–8 KB calibration text per domain. The cost is one counting pass plus a `2¹⁸×23` matvec—a ∼23× reduction in forward cost*"

(with the honest unit correction "*The `23` classes are cumulative-prefix *territories*, not languages*"). The disagreement analysis is the paper's most subtle result: "*hi routes to bg/el under likelihood but to Latin territories under the byte fit … neither is wrong … the cheap addresser faithfully reports *distributional proximity*; the likelihood router answers a different question, *realized fit**", with a zero-parameter discriminator "*Maximum-centroid cosine … Latin-family inputs at 0.78–0.85, cross-script inputs at 0.07–0.14*" and its cost "*this trigger escalates 4 of 7 unseen inputs where catching the divergences needs 2*". Self-audit: "*The margin floor itself is optimistic: it comes from a fit that was 160/160 correct on those same crops*".

**F8 — Out-of-support detection needs two axes.**
> "*For trained languages the advantage is 5.7–15.6× … lv 0.98× … ga 1.39× … But for the cross-script languages the ratio *inflates* past the trained floor: zh 3.87×, ja 3.21×, hi 5.74× … A pure ratio threshold does not separate cross-script unseen from trained.*"

Two-axis rule: "*reject routing when the best-route perplexity exceeds ∼10× the acquisition band (2.36–6.47) … all 20 trained languages pass and all 6 unseen reject*" — with the fit/eval caveat quoted in §3.2 above. All ratio values verified against `2026-09-11_cross-script-rejection-suite.md` ✓ (including iu-clean best-route 303.61 → "304").

**F9 — Cross-script routing is byte geometry, not linguistics.**
> "*Chinese, Japanese, Hindi, and Inuktitut route 37–40/40 to the only two territories with substantial multi-byte training exposure (Cyrillic and Greek: 82% of characters are 2-byte-encoded, against 11.7% for the next-highest territory, a factor of 7), with zero linguistic kinship*"

and the mechanism exclusion: "*bg and el win cross-script inputs not because they have seen those scripts—nobody has—but because they are the only territories whose weights live in a multi-byte regime at all*". Falsifier status: "*the pre-registered falsification case (Latin-route attraction) never occurred*" (post-correction). The iu contamination disclosure ("*the original Inuktitut file mixed syllabic text with Turkish film-subtitle lines … (454 of 719 lines Latin)*"; routing "*strengthens … from 25/40 to 40/40*") is verified against the rejection-suite report ✓ (which also records "*4.64× → 1.90×*" for iu — both numbers are in the paper, correctly).

**F10 — The cross-script growth cell preserves, routes, and serves across script families.**
> "*a Chinese territory grown on an English base, then Devanagari grown on Chinese, retains all three bit-exactly, routes perfectly, and serves each at acquisition quality*"

with the scoped claim "*This is the tested cross-script cell (two growth transitions): it shows the construction remained functional across script families, not that every future transition is guaranteed*". Verified: 2.78/2.81/2.79/23.92/8.5× all match the cross-script report ✓.

## 5. Limitations & Biases

### 5.1 Limitations the authors state (well done)

The Limitations and Open Problems sections are unusually candid: "*single seed per run (measured seed floor 2–4%); one language ordering per ladder … byte-level models only … 100M–579M parameters on two GPUs of one team; rejection thresholds empirical for this checkpoint and instrument, with conformal freezing future work; twenty growth phases; and the Inuktitut data reality (163 KB total public holding)*"; and the architecture-vs-objective confound "*moves two variables at once … Isolating them is a *follow-up project**". The separation of "*Results we did not explain*" (bg/el asymmetry — "*bg and el have similar acquisition exits (`6.09` and `6.36`) … yet the trained ladder loses `37.5×` at bg against `9.8×` at el*"; the multimodality mechanism; the residual damage — "*We have not separated those two terms experimentally*") from scope gaps is exemplary reporting.

### 5.2 Limitations missed or under-weighted by the authors

1. **Seed × order × batch-size interaction is unquantified.** The paper treats the 2–4% seed floor as if it were the only run-level variance, but `ra2b_acquisition.csv` reveals an undisclosed 4× token-budget drop mid-ladder (batch 4 / 20.48M tokens for phases 1–4 → batch 1 / 5.12M for phases 5–20). Any per-phase cost/acquisition trend across the chain is therefore confounded with a training-budget change the paper never mentions. This deserves at least a protocol footnote; ideally a sensitivity run.

2. **Circularity risk in the +4.3% headline.** The retention section argues the escape hatch is closed: "*The instrument-offset escape is removed on provenance grounds, not replaced by a substitute measurement*" and "*the sixteen matrix diagonal cells that agree with the logged exits do so because both come from the same random-crop evaluation family … their agreement is cross-implementation consistency rather than evidence about a window-versus-val offset*". But note what the paper is doing: it *rejects* the diagonal cells as evidence for instrument offset (because same family) while *relying* on the same-family property to claim "+4.3% is the routing cost, not an instrument difference" ("*What makes the retained number strong is exactly that routed and acquisition serving are measured under the same evaluation family*"). The argument is not formally circular — routed-vs-exit is same-family by design — but the exits come from the "*training-window crop*" per `a4_summary.json`'s own denominator note ("*per-language exit_ppl from 2026-09-05_ra2b_acquisition.csv (training-window crop)*") while routed values come from the routing diagnostic ("*own-prefix routed ppl from ladRA2b_routdiag_p20.txt*"). Same evaluation *family*, different windows and crop seeds. The residual +4.3% could be (in part) window offset; the paper asserts it is "*a measured quality difference, not instrumentation*" without the one experiment that would decide it (identical crops, routed vs. exit, same script). Given the median is 4.3% — barely above the admitted 2–4% seed floor — this matters.

3. **Likelihood-router cost is never measured as a serving cost.** The paper measures acquisition cost, routed cost, restore overhead ("*0.59% of step time*"), and the cheap addresser's "*23× reduction in forward cost versus scanning all widths*" — but the baseline being reduced (the full-width NLL scan over 20 prefixes on 128 tokens) is never quantified in latency/FLOPs/cost. For a paper whose thesis is "*stable, efficient **addressing***", the efficiency axis of the central claim is asserted through the 23× ratio rather than measured end-to-end.

4. **"Oracle serving" admission understates its own consequence.** "*At 20/20 domains with 40/40 crops each, the observed routing is therefore *oracle serving* for this benchmark: per-sample selection buys nothing that a fixed correct assignment would not also give*" is refreshingly honest — but it means the routed-vs-acquisition comparison (+4.3%) does not measure *router-induced* serving loss at all, only prefix-masking loss. The two are conflated under "routing cost". If the router ever errs (as it does for hi/iu under the cheap addresser), the cost is unmeasured here.

5. **Evaluation corpus proximity.** Every domain is parallel-corpus register ("*all domains from parallel-corpus-adjacent registers*"), and the paper admits the untested "*'domain' notion for chat-level continual learning*". But the byte-geometry thesis is at its most fragile exactly where domains share registers and scripts (e.g., code vs. prose in one script; formal vs. colloquial register in one language). "*Nothing here measures whether the same address geometry survives in domains whose statistics are not separable at the byte level*" — acknowledged, but the abstract's "byte-separable in the regime measured here" is doing more load-bearing work than casual readers will notice.

6. **Theorem-level nit already covered in §3.3** (Theorem 1 proof step; Theorem 2 statement indices) — flagged here as a limitation of the theory's current rigor even though the results are believable.

7. **Publication-date claims include contemporaneous artifacts that cannot be independently checked.** The DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash Engram citation ("*released 2026-09-10; accessed 2026-09 … Cited as vendor documentation, not peer-reviewed work*") and the Marin tracker thread ("*thread 8818 … accessed 2026-09*") are correctly labeled non-peer-reviewed, but the precedent argument ("*suggesting a failure class, not an idiosyncrasy of our setup*") leans on an ephemeral tracker thread that may not be durably archived.

### 5.3 Potential biases

- **Single-lab, single-architect confirmation risk.** All instruments were built by the same AI-agent team that built the system under test. The mitigations are real (independent second seat, self-caught withdrawals, pre-registration), but there is no external replication. The paper's own discipline ("*The withdrawal sequence is itself evidence for the process discipline this project runs on*") should not substitute for external audit.
- **Narrative-selection bias in the arc.** The measurement arc is ordered so each section resolves the previous one's tension (forgetting → decay confound → preservation → readout mechanics → selection). This is good pedagogy but shapes perception: the negative/qualified results (P-R1 NEGATIVE, P-R3 initial withdrawal, era-7 falsifier trigger, iu contamination) are distributed into sub-sections while positive results headline. The pre-register table largely corrects this; a skeptical listing of all failed/post-hoc-changed analyses in the appendix would correct it fully.
- **The "operator" framing.** Phrases like "*the operator asked for it explicitly (pre-registered as P-FCS-1–3)*" and "*the operator's `20 → 20,000` question*" introduce an unusual external-principal voice in an archival paper. It reads as internal-project provenance leaking into scholarly voice; recommend neutral rewording in a journal version.

## 6. Scholarly Context

The paper engages prior work in unusual depth ("*Short citations are not sufficient; we show the engagement*"), and the engagement is substantive rather than decorative. Positioned against the field:

- **Additive/parameter-isolating growth** (Progressive Networks, PackNet, Piggyback, SupSup): the paper claims a genuine delta — "*their columns were never summed into one readout*" — i.e., the storage/serving dissociation is invisible in architectures with per-task decoupled readouts and arises specifically from BDH's shared summation readout. This is a fair and non-trivial distinction; the random-expansion control instantiates exactly the failure mode within-budget masks "*can [neither] create nor repair*". The claim "*our contribution is the storage mathematics under a shared readout, which is exactly where their story ends and ours begins*" is defensible.
- **Task addressing** (Expert Gate, L2P): the label-free self-NLL selector ("*our selector is label-free (arg-min NLL by the model itself)*") and the measured rejection geometry are credible advances over label-requiring per-task autoencoders. The non-unimodality finding ("*selection is robust but not sublinear*") is, to my knowledge, a new empirical fact about cumulative-prefix likelihood surfaces and is the most citable negative result in the paper.
- **Consolidation** (EWC/SI/MAS): the regime-boundary claim — "*in our growth regime there is nothing for importance protection to do: gradients never reach old segments (the mask is exact), so the Fisher matrix over old parameters is identically zero over the preserved trajectory—a measured null, not an omission*" — is correct in spirit (though "identically zero" follows by construction from the mask and is not itself a measurement).
- **Replay** (GEM/A-GEM): the paper's most useful comparative datum is its own H1p result — "*replay … reached joint parity … at `+27%` additional training budget in the fixed-capacity regime*" — yielding the cost-allocation statement "*Replay pays data and compute; growth pays serving and addressing*". This is the kind of exchange-rate statement the CL literature rarely quantifies.
- **MoE routing** (Switch/GShard): the paper correctly identifies the structural difference (learned during-training routing with load balance vs. structural prefix territories solved at serving time) and adds "*Our two-axis rejection has no MoE analogue*" — fair, since MoE operates in-support by construction.
- **Selective prediction** (Chow, conformal): the two-axis rule is positioned as "*Chow's rule with a measured twist—confidence alone (the ratio) is insufficient, because 'confidently least-wrong' is not 'in support'*". The conformal gap is honestly declared (P-R4 NOT STARTED). This is the right lineage; the specific degeneration of a ratio statistic under cross-script catastrophe is a new empirical caution for reject-option work in generative models.
- **Production-scale conditional memory** (DeepSeek Engram): "*the closest public prior art at production scale; it establishes that the substrate idea is deployable and leaves the accumulation question—ours to answer*" — a reasonable division of labor, with correct caveats that vendor cards establish deployability, not continual-learning behavior.
- **CL evaluation methodology**: the storage/serving/addressing/support decomposition and the two-arm re-acquisition probe ("*operationalizes 'destroyed vs. inaccessible'—a distinction standard benchmarks cannot make*") are the paper's most portable contributions and stand independent of BDH itself.

**Where the paper over-reaches contextually:** the title thesis ("not destructive forgetting") is established for the *growth regime* only, while forgetting of the fixed-capacity kind remains precisely destructive (their own FCS result). The claim is true of the construction under test, not of continual learning writ large; the paper body scopes this correctly ("*Scope: fixed capacity only—the growth regime is the opposite*") but the title and abstract invite the broader reading.

## 7. Critical Q&A

**Q1 (design confound): The fixed-capacity floor and the growth ladder differ in both capacity mechanism and training objective (route-aware loss, α=0.9). How can you attribute preservation to the architecture?**
*Suggested answer (from the text):* We cannot fully separate them, and we say so: the contrast "*moves two variables at once: the capacity mechanism *and* the route-aware auxiliary loss … therefore cannot separate how much of the recovered routed-serving competence is bought by the architecture and how much by the objective*". Importantly, the *storage* claim does not depend on the objective: bit-exactness follows from gradient masking plus step-end restore ("*old-segment tensor equality `c=1.000000`*") and is verified at the tensor level regardless of loss. The confound bears on routed-serving competence, and we specify the isolating arm ("*the same growth, masking and restore, with `route_aware=False`*") as follow-up work. *Reviewer assessment: acceptable for a measurement paper, but this arm should precede any journal version's headline claim.*

**Q2 (statistics): All headline numbers are single-run; your own measured seed floor is 2–4%, and the retention headline (+4.3%) sits barely above it. Why should we believe the routed-cost band is not seed noise?**
*Suggested answer:* The band's credibility does not rest on the aggregate median alone: (i) the p19→p20 comparison is *bit-identical* across a full growth phase ("*zero drift … against `+88%` (fi) and `+21%` (hu) in the leaky era*"), a within-run comparison immune to seed variance; (ii) the per-domain pattern is systematic (20/20 domains ≤ +8%, with the worst at hu and the best slightly negative at fr — verified in `a4_routed_cost.csv`), not scattered around a noisy center; (iii) routed and acquisition values share one evaluation family ("*`+4.3%` is the routing cost, not an instrument difference*"). We do claim multi-seed repetition as future work ("*a multi-seed repetition of the headline retention and routing numbers*"). *Reviewer assessment: the bit-identical drift result is the load-bearing defense and it is strong; the +4.3% point estimate itself should be quoted with the seed-floor caveat attached everywhere.*

**Q3 (OOD validity): Your reject thresholds are fit and evaluated on the same n=6 probe set. What licenses any claim of out-of-support detection?**
*Suggested answer:* Only a measured-separation claim, and we state exactly that: "*the thresholds are fit and evaluated on the same `n=6` probe set: a measured separation, not held-out validation*" and "*Independent unseen languages must be collected and the rule re-tested before it is used for any real rejection decision.*" The structural finding survives threshold-fitting: the *ratio statistic degenerates* under cross-script catastrophe (hi 5.74× breaches the trained floor) while absolute perplexity separates by orders of magnitude (304–732 vs. 2.36–6.47) — that two-axis *structure* is not a fitted artifact, even if its numeric cutoffs are. Conformal freezing (P-R4) is the declared path to calibrated thresholds. *Reviewer assessment: the honest answer; the abstract's "measured separation, not held-out validation" parenthetical must stay.*

**Q4 (reproducibility): The paper says "*Every number in this paper traces to a committed artifact*", yet the Markdown version drops the seven-operator table, a source citation renders as "extttdocs/…", the FCS matrix and byte census are not in `data/`, and `rev4-ai-disclosure-draft.md` is cited as "committed with this revision" but is not. Which artifact set reproduces Section 6.2?**
*Suggested answer:* The TeX source carries the full operator table (absK, ev-shift, block-average, log-norm, massnorm, softmix, calibgain rows with per-language values) and the built PDF is authoritative ("*the built PDF is the final authority*"); the Markdown sync is secondary and should be regenerated. The underlying numbers trace to `docs/reports/2026-09-11_pi-50_expansion-control-and-readout-operators.md` (P-R1) and the P-R1b run logs. We will (a) regenerate the Markdown from the final TeX, (b) fix the `\texttt` tab-corruption, (c) move the FCS matrix, byte census, and `ladRA2b_routdiag_p20.txt` into `docs/papers/rev5/data/` with manifest hashes, and (d) commit the rev5 disclosure document. *Reviewer assessment: these are fixable packaging defects, not evidentiary ones — but as of this revision the paper's reproduction promise is not met on its own terms.*

**Q5 (theory rigor): Theorem 1's proof asserts `F'^L(x) = F_A^L(x) + Lc1` without assuming translation equivariance of `F_A`, and Theorem 2's statement quantifies `0≤ℓ≤L` with a free `ℓ`. Are the results correct?**
*Suggested answer:* The results are correct but the presentation is defective. For Theorem 1, the construction can be repaired directly: take the suffix to add its constant at *every* level invocation by defining `F'(h) = F_A(h) + c·1` compositionally (the extended map is applied per level, so the shift is `c` per level *by construction* of `F'`, not by equivariance of `F_A`), or use a specialist with constant trajectories where the arithmetic is trivial. For Theorem 2, the intended statement quantifies over every truncation depth `ℓ` ("*The hypothesis at `L=ℓ*` gives …*" uses exactly this), and we will restate it as "for all `x` and all `ℓ≤L`". *Reviewer assessment: correctable in a page of edits; fix before journal submission — theorem statements with free indices and unlicensed equivariance steps will not survive a mathematical reviewer.*

## 8. Summary Judgment & Recommended Improvements

**Overall assessment:** A strong, unusually honest measurement paper with exemplary failure-disclosure practice, a real methodological contribution (the storage/serving/addressing/support decomposition and the two-arm re-acquisition probe), and a reproducible headline arithmetic record (of 21 checked number-groups, I reproduced 18 exactly, found 2 not verifiable from shipped artifacts, and 1 wrong). The weaknesses — single-seed/one-order design, the architecture-vs-objective confound, the n=6 fit-and-eval OOD rule, and packaging defects in the Markdown/TeX sync — are mostly acknowledged by the author themselves. **Recommendation: accept subject to revision** for a replication-friendly venue (e.g., a machine-learning systems or continual-learning workshop track), with the following required revisions; a journal version needs the isolating arm (Q1) and multi-seed/order runs first.

**Required fixes (packaging/numerical):**
1. Correct the f32-offset sentence in §2.3 — the gap `0.892752 − 0.892636 = 1.16×10⁻⁴` is accumulated **per step** (~9,700 plateau steps × ≈1.36×10⁻⁸), not "−1.4×10⁻⁶ per plateau phase"; reconcile the quoted constant and its unit with `2026-09-04_decay-family-two-regimes-and-boundary-repair.md` ("*matching the measured −1.303e-4 to +1.4e-6*").
2. Regenerate the Markdown sync from the final TeX so §6.2 contains the seven-operator table; repair the tab-corrupted `\texttt` citation ("*extttdocs/reports/2026-09-13_quinn_tier1-router-split-report.md*"); fix the duplicated register header row.
3. Unify the four operationalizations of "acquisition band" (1.54–2.29 FCS exits; 2.25–5.99 RA2b best-val; 2.36–6.47 routed p20 mislabeled "acquisition" in §7.3; test-vs-best-val mixed for zh 2.69/2.48) — pick one term per measurement and restate the OOD threshold against the correct band.
4. Commit the promised rev5 `ai-disclosure` document and the raw FCS 21×20 matrix, byte census, `ladRA2b_routdiag_p20.txt`, and OOD probe table into `data/` with manifest hashes (the manifest hash mechanism already exists and works).
5. Disclose the mid-ladder batch/token change (phases 1–4 batch 4 / 20.48M tokens → phases 5–20 batch 1 / 5.12M) in §2.2, and reconcile "four orders" (abstract) with the source report's "five orders" for bg/el collapse.

**Recommended improvements (science):**
6. Run the isolating arm (growth + restore with `route_aware=False`) — the paper's own design — and a 3-seed × 2-order replication of the retention/routing headlines before any journal submission.
7. Replace the fit-and-eval OOD cutoffs with the conformal protocol already specified (P-R4): freeze thresholds on held-out labeled crops before touching exotic test sets; add independent unseen languages to break the n=6 circularity.
8. Decide the +4.3% window-vs-routing question with the decisive experiment: routed and exit perplexity on the *identical* crop set and script. Until then, quote "+4.3% (median; at the 2–4% seed floor)".
9. Measure the addressing cost end-to-end (likelihood-scan latency vs. byte-addresser prefilter vs. oracle) — the thesis is about *efficient* addressing, and the efficiency claim currently rests on one 23× ratio.
10. Repair Theorem 1's proof step (construct `F'` to shift per level by definition, or assume/prove the needed equivariance) and restate Theorem 2 with the truncated-depth quantifier made explicit.
11. Tone-map provenance voice ("*the operator asked for it explicitly*", "*the operator's `20 → 20,000` question*") into neutral scholarly phrasing in any external submission; move project-process narrative to the disclosure document.

**Closing note.** The paper's most citable durable contributions are likely to be: (a) the dissociation framework (storage/serving/addressing/support) as portable CL methodology; (b) the decay-leak failure class and its closed-form derivation ("*suggesting a failure class, not an idiosyncrasy*"); (c) the non-unimodality result blocking sublinear prefix search; and (d) the two-axis rejection structure with its measured ratio degeneration. Each stands independent of BDH's own performance numbers — which is the mark of a good measurement paper.
