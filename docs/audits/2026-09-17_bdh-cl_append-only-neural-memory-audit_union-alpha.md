# Audit: "Append-Only Neural Memory" (Buchholz, rev 4.6) — mathematical & internal-consistency findings

Scope: the full TeX of Revision 4.6 as supplied in conversation. Same method as the BDH audit: every headline number recomputed where derivable, every proof checked line by line, empirical claims checked for internal consistency (cross-section, figure vs. text, table vs. table). Findings separated into (A) demonstrable errors, (B) proof gaps, (C) internal inconsistencies, (D) unsupported/overclaimed conclusions, (E) verified items.

## A. Demonstrable errors

1. **Proposition prop:soft — wrong constant in the functional equation.** The paper states the preservation requirement as $(1{+}g_1)^2 + g_1g_2\varepsilon\delta = 4$ "for all input magnitudes $a>0$". Redoing the derivation from the paper's own one-step state $x_1 = (a(1{+}g_1),\, g_2\delta a)$ and the two-step first-component condition $a(1{+}g_1) + g_1\sigma(a(1{+}g_1)) + g_1\varepsilon g_2\delta a = a$ (valid on the branch $1{+}g_1>0$, where $\sigma(a(1{+}g_1)) = a(1{+}g_1)$) gives
   $$(1{+}g_1)^2 + g_1g_2\varepsilon\delta = 1,$$
   not $4$. The conclusion (no input-independent solution; hard endpoint forced, $g_1=g_2=0$) survives — indeed with $g_2=0$ the equation reduces to $(1{+}g_1)^2=1$, i.e. $g_1=0$ in the open range, which matches the paper's own later reduction $g_1(1+\sigma(a(1{+}g_1)))=0$. But the displayed equation as printed is false, and a reader who tries to use "$=4$" will find the proposition unprovable as stated. Additionally, the derivation silently assumes the positive-ReLU branch throughout ($1+g_1>0$); the negative branch changes the equation, so the "for all $a>0$" universal quantifier is doing unacknowledged work.

2. **Section sec:decay — the reconciliation arithmetic contradicts the stated float-offset size.** The paper says the f32 realization "adds a deterministic offset of $-1.4\times10^{-6}$ per plateau phase" reconciling measured $0.892636$ with exact $0.892752$. The gap is $1.16\times10^{-4}$, i.e. $\approx 83\times$ larger than $1.4\times10^{-6}$. Even multiplying the offset by the full $10{,}000$-step schedule gives $1.4\times10^{-2}$, which is $100\times$ too large for a per-phase effect — the units of the offset are wrong (per step vs. per phase vs. cumulative is never made consistent). The abstract's stronger claim that the closed form is "verified … to five decimal places" is directly contradicted by the paper's own numbers: $|0.892636-0.892752| = 1.16\times10^{-4}$ — agreement only to **four** decimal places. (Body text is more careful — "residuals $\sim10^{-5}$" — but even that is inconsistent with the two quoted six-decimal values.)

3. **Figure fig:fcs caption vs. text denominator.** Caption: "9/19 fully erased". Text (Section sec:fcs): "nine of sixteen comparable domains". $9/19 \ne 9/16$; the nineteen presumably counts Latin-script languages but there are not nineteen Latin-script languages in the listed 20 (en, es, pl, fr, de, cs, da, pt, fi, hu, bg, it, et, el, sk, sv, ro, nl, sl, lt — bg/el are Cyrillic/Greek, so 17–18 Latin-script at most, and "comparable" is defined as 16). One of the two denominators is wrong; and the language lists themselves leave **lt** (Lithuanian) unaccounted for in the row-20 split: 9 displaced + 7 partial + bg + el = 18 of 20; only en (reference) explains one, leaving lt's disposition unstated anywhere in the section.

## B. Proof gaps

4. **Theorem thm:criterion, ($\Rightarrow$) direction — unproven trajectory reachability.** The proof requires "choose $x\in\Xa$ whose trajectory passes through $z^*$ at depth $\ell^* < L$ (such $x$ exists by definition of $\TA$)". The definition of $\TA = \bigcup_{\ell\le L}\{F_A^\ell(x)\}$ guarantees a *witness* $x$, but the induction hypothesis was only established along the trajectory of the *same* $x$; to apply the hypothesis "for all $x$" at depth $\ell^*+1$ is fine, but the set equality $F'^\ell = F_A^\ell$ must first hold on all of $\Xa$ at *every* depth — which is exactly the induction being run. The argument is repairable (strong induction over depth with $x$ re-chosen per depth) but as written it begd the question by mixing two different witnesses. Minor but real gap in a "PROVED"-stamped theorem.

5. **Corollary cor:prefix — "LayerNorm's global statistics break (C1) at first order in the suffix magnitude" carries no proof, no constant, and no regime.** This is the load-bearing bridge from the exact theory to the measured joint-serving failure (the entire Section sec:readout narrative assumes the soft-perturbation picture). It is stated with "PROVED" in the corollary header but the appendix contains no proof of it, only the empirics of Remark rem:exp and Appendix app:meas. Should be marked MEASURED/HEURISTIC.

6. **Proposition prop:amp — scope of "uniform-in-depth bounding requires contraction" is not established.** The proof shows the trivial $(1+L_f)$ recursion; the contraction claim "$\rho<1$ on the interference dynamics yields $\delta_{\max}/(1-\rho)$" is asserted without defining the interference dynamics, the norm, or showing the recursion closes under it. Given that Remark rem:exp then measures *against* this premise (directional gains 1.05–1.89), the proposition is doing rhetorical work its proof cannot support — the honest statement is only the $(1+L_f)$ bound, which is vacuous for $L_f>0$ at depth 20–736.

## C. Internal inconsistencies (numbers vs. numbers)

7. **"Five orders of magnitude" collapse is four.** bg: $\log_{10}(18613/1.54) = 4.08$; el: $\log_{10}(10928/1.59) = 3.84$. The abstract, intro, and Section sec:fcs all say "collapse by five orders of magnitude"; the paper's own acquisition numbers give four (bg) and just under four (el). Even against the most favorable denominators this does not reach five.

8. **Four vs. five confirmations.** "Bit-exactness: four independent confirmations" (Section sec:ra2b) then lists five transitions: en→es, es→pl, p19→p20, the 4090 fixed-capacity chain, and zh→hi. The abstract says "verified four independent ways, including cross-script" — the enumerated list and both counts never reconcile (five items, two hosts, two scripts).

9. **Acquisition-band drift across sections.** Section sec:fcs fixes the European acquisition band at 1.54–2.29. Section sec:xscript measures zh at 2.69 (test 2.48) and hi at 2.78 — both *above* the band, acknowledged only implicitly ("script-agnostic", no comment on the band). Section sec:ood then quotes an "acquisition band (2.36–6.47)" — a third band whose lower edge (2.36) matches neither 1.54–2.29 nor any quoted routed number, and whose derivation (routed? which chain?) is never given. The OOD absolute-axis threshold ($\sim10\times$ the band) is therefore anchored to an unexplained interval.

10. **Identity-control drift between tables.** The expansion control (Section sec:readout) has "none (base) = 2.33"; the readout-operator table's identity control is 32.04 (en) — a $13.7\times$ difference between two "identity" instruments, explained only by a footnote about per-run controls and no statement of why the two protocols differ by more than an order of magnitude on the same base. The cross-run comparison discipline is stated, but a reviewer must take on faith that the 83%-arithmetic claim (computed against 2.33/31.07) and the operator-refutation claims (computed against 32.04) describe the same model. (Verified internally consistent where recomputable: $\ln(20.16/2.33)/\ln(31.07/2.33) = 0.833$; linear $(20.16-2.33)/(31.07-2.33) = 0.620$; one-block $9.57/2.33 = 4.11\times$ — all match.)

11. **remark rem:exp amplification claim vs. its own table.** The text claims per-level amplification "$\sim1.2$–$1.5\times$" but the ungated row of the table gives successive ratios $0.4816/0.4024 = 1.20,\ 0.6340/0.4816 = 1.32,\ 0.7697/0.6340 = 1.21,\ 0.8580/0.7697 = 1.11,\ 0.9078/0.8580 = 1.06$ — the amplification *decays* and the last ratio is 1.06, outside the stated range. "Saturates" is the right word; "1.2–1.5×" is not supported by the printed table.

## D. Unsupported / overclaimed conclusions

12. **The headline "83% arithmetic" is a single-checkpoint generalization.** The paper is admirably careful in Section sec:readout (fractions 0.599–1.472 over seven eras; "the fraction does not [generalize]"), but the abstract, introduction, discussion, and conclusion all repeat "83%/62%" as *the* characterization of joint degradation, with the era-dependence relegated to a parenthetical. Since the fraction exceeds 1 in one era and dips to 0.60 in another, the honest summary statistic is a range, not a point; the abstract's "the same mechanism reproduces the majority of the damage in six [of seven]" is the accurate sentence, and it is not the one used in the conclusion ("83% on the log scale (62% in linear perplexity)" restated bare).

13. **"No language-agnostic scalar reweighting repairs growth damage" — tested space is seven operators, two vacuous, one (calibgain) that is itself input-dependent-by-fitting.** The paper hedges correctly in-line ("we do not claim the space … is exhausted"), but the Discussion asserts "repairable by no fixed operator we could construct" as falsifier (ii) and the Consequence paragraph universalizes over "every fixed arithmetic remedy". Given (a) $n=7$ families, (b) two of them provably vacuous, (c) calibgain's fitted vector converging to *oracle masking* (i.e., the family arguably contains the correct answer at its boundary — a per-input selector), the universal negative is an overclaim from the evidence. The paper even concedes the counterexample in-line ("a sufficiently expressive fixed map can itself implement input-dependent behaviour"), which contradicts the falsifier statement.

14. **The decay-confound closed form is schedule-specific and the verification is circular-adjacent.** The factor $c = \prod(1-\mathrm{lr}_t\cdot\mathrm{wd})$ is verified against measured residuals on "every checkpoint transition we tested" — transitions from the *same* schedule family whose product defines $c$. The three "independent instruments" (c-fits, moment census, spectral fingerprints) all measure the same multiplicative shrinkage; agreement among them confirms the mechanism but not the closed form's independence from data (the "independent of data, loss, or routing" clause is untested — no experiment varies data at fixed schedule). Also the schedule described in the text ("warmup 30, decay 300, plateau at $10^{-4}$ for 97% of steps") does not match the Protocol table ("warmup 1000, cosine to $\min$ over 10,000 iters") — presumably the two schedules from the two arms, but the text presents the plateau schedule's $c=0.8927$ and the cosine's $c=0.5798$ without saying which experiments ran which; my rough integration of the stated schedules gives $c \approx 0.8925$ (plateau) and $c\approx0.577$ (cosine), consistent with the quoted values — the mechanism is fine, the bookkeeping of *which ladder had which defect* is not recoverable from the text.

15. **OOD thresholds: fit and evaluated on the same $n=6$ probe set.** The paper discloses this itself ("measured, but it is not held-out validation") — commended — but the abstract still reports "a two-axis rule separates 20 trained from 6 unseen" as a result without the disclosure. With 2 free thresholds and 6 unseen probes, the separation is a description of the probe set, not a validated rejection rule. Similarly the byte-addresser's margin floor "comes from a fit that was 160/160 correct on those same crops" (disclosed in-line, absent from abstract).

16. **Routing "20/20 … 40/40 crops" significance.** The paper's own Wilson-floor discussion is good practice, but the online-routing caveat ("oracle serving") is buried mid-paragraph in sec:ra2b while the abstract headlines "20/20 domains routing perfectly". The held-out-split confirmation (quinn tier1 report, bdh/2166c24) is cited but its sample size and Wilson band are not given, so the strongest form of the routing claim rests on the non-held-out protocol.

17. **Cross-script routing "37–40/40 … zero linguistic kinship"** — verified consistent (zh 37, ja/hi/iu 40; 82%/11.7% byte census gives the stated factor 7.0). The scoped statement ("no linguistic reading is required") is fine; but zh 37/40 means 3 crops went *somewhere else*, and the destination of those 3 crops is never reported — the one datum that would test the "high-byte attraction" mechanism against a counterexample is omitted.

## E. Verified as correct (context)

- Expansion-control arithmetic: $f_{\log} = 0.833$, $f_{\mathrm{lin}} = 0.620$, one-block $4.11\times$ — all match the quoted table exactly; the seven-era own-point reproduction ($20.08$ vs $20.16$, $-0.4\%$) is arithmetically consistent.
- AdamW decay mechanism: gradient-masking does not shield decoupled weight decay (a zero gradient still triggers the $(1-\mathrm{lr}\cdot\mathrm{wd})$ factor) — correct and a genuinely useful observation; my schedule integration reproduces $c\approx0.8925$/$0.577$ vs. quoted $0.8927$/$0.5798$ (schedule details approximate).
- Wilson floors: 40/40 → $[0.912, 1]$ (quoted 0.91 ✓); 8/8 → $[0.676, 1]$ (quoted 0.68 ✓).
- Theorem thm:dissoc and its proof: correct as an existence statement (constant suffix shift $c\mathbf 1$; $F'^L = F_A^L + Lc\mathbf 1$); the "conversely" clause is fine. Note the construction is trivially linear — the theorem is a valid but weak demonstration (it does not involve ReLU cross-coupling; the interesting version is prop:soft, where the error in item A.1 lives).
- Lemma lem:zf (zero-forcing) and its proof: correct.
- Proposition prop:amp recursion: the $(1+L_f)$ bound itself is correctly derived (see B.6 for the contraction add-on).
- Green-arrow percentages: $1-3.72/9.94 = 62.6\%$, $1-3.36/9.45 = 64.4\%$ (quoted $-63\%$, $-64\%$ ✓, first rounds poorly).
- Joint recovery figures: $230/6.09 = 37.8\times$ bg ✓ (and the 37.8× worst-case in fig:retention is this same bg number ✓).
- Cross-script cell: zh retention $2.81/2.69 = +4.5\%$ ✓; advantage $23.92/2.81 = 8.5\times$ ✓.
- Byte census factor: $82/11.7 = 7.0$ ✓; restore-cost $17.9/3038 = 0.59\%$ ✓; Engram scale $196{,}000/579 \approx 339$ ≈ "$340\times$" ✓; "six of seven eras majority" ↔ min fraction $0.599 > 0.5$ ✓; 23 router classes = 4 base sub-widths + 19 grown ✓; iu contamination $719 - 454 = 265$ syllabic lines ✓; 8 crops × 512 B = 4 KB smallest calibration budget ✓.
- The storage/serving/addressing decomposition, the FCS fixed-capacity baseline design, the two-arm re-acquisition probe with its disclosed resolution caveat, and the unusually high density of in-line negative results and disclosed corrections (withdrawn 76% addresser fit, iu contamination, instrument-offset removal) are methodologically strong; the paper's self-auditing culture is well above field norm.

## Summary

The empirical spine (bit-exact storage under masked growth; arithmetic readout damage; input-dependent selection as the only working repair; two-axis OOD) is internally coherent and most recomputable numbers check out. The defects concentrate in: (i) the theory section's stamped-but-unproven soft-perturbation bridge (cor:prefix) and a wrong constant in prop:soft's displayed equation; (ii) the decay-confound reconciliation, whose own numbers contradict both the stated offset and the abstract's "five decimal places"; (iii) a cluster of small but real counting/denominator errors ("five orders", 4-vs-5 confirmations, 9/19 vs 9/16, three different "acquisition bands"); and (iv) universal-negative claims (no fixed operator repairs; 83% as a portable fraction) that outrun the seven-operator, single-checkpoint evidence, with the honest qualifications present in body text but stripped from abstract and conclusion.
