## 1. Probe R — matched-information residual readout

Question: does a residual head stay inferior to the byte baseline when both read the same
96-byte crops and fit on the same 56 training crops per language (20 RA2b-lt Europarl
territories, 480 held-out crops)?

| method | test accuracy |
|---|---:|
| byte 4-gram (domain-supervised, train-only modal route map) | **479/480 = 0.9979** (Wilson 0.9883–0.9996) |
| best head (L1-mean-mlp256, validation-selected) | **456/480 = 0.9500** (Wilson 0.9267–0.9662) |
| descriptive domain-oracle route map | 479/480 = 0.9979 |

McNemar discordants: **23 byte-only correct, 0 head-only** — the gap is one-sided.

Readout structure (validation table, all twelve variants):

- Mean pooling outperformed the last-state readout in every tested layer/architecture
  pair on validation: L1-mean 0.93125 versus L1-last 0.28125 (MLP-256);
  L3-mean 0.90625 versus L3-last 0.221875. This establishes a readout-performance
  difference under this fitting recipe, not where information resides.
- The validation-selected variant was L1-mean-mlp256. These comparisons do not
  establish a general depth law or exclude other effective last-state readouts.

Interpretation bounds: this is a new controlled comparison (96-byte crops, fresh split,
domain-supervised byte baseline), **not** a causal decomposition of the earlier
93.10 % vs 100 % result — crop length, teacher window and split differ. The conclusion
that survives: under matched inputs and matched training budget, a shallow residual
readout still loses to byte statistics. There is no accuracy-based reason here to
replace the byte comparator with this head. The byte comparator remains an
in-distribution candidate; serving suitability and latency were not evaluated.

## 2. Probe J — independently calibrated byte rejection

Question: can a fixed threshold on minimum-class NLL per byte, calibrated on a
**disjoint** calibration region, retain trained languages while rejecting fresh text
regions of unseen languages? (P-C4′'s ratio-axis construction had failed; J replaces it
pre-registered with a single primary axis. The six P-C4′ languages are development
languages; J samples fresh regions plus a 1-MiB guard.)

- Frozen threshold: tau = 4.0380 NLL/byte (99th percentile, 1920 calibration crops).
- **J-1 PASS**: independent ID-test acceptance **1919/1920 = 0.9995** (pl 95/96;
  all other languages 96/96). Domain classification on accepted crops was 100%.
- **J-2 PASS on five available cells**: OOD mean-NLL acceptance **0/5**; crop-level
  false-acceptance **0.0 % for ga, hi, ja, lv, zh** (each 96 crops).
- **iu BLOCKED**: the clean Inuktitut source (126,144 bytes) has no region outside the
  development tail + guard. Suite status INCOMPLETE, not COMPLETE.

Interpretation bounds: fresh regions of known development languages, not untouched
languages; byte-disjoint regions do not establish document independence. J supports
this fixed byte-NLL rejection rule on the measured sample. Zero observed false
acceptances in 96 crops per language does not establish a zero population rate.
No likelihood fallback was evaluated. Production serving, novelty detection and
transfer to genuinely new language families remain unvalidated.
